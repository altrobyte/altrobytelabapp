You can read an analog sensor with the ESP32's ADC and dim an LED with PWM.

ADC: voltage to a number

  • Sensors like a light sensor or a potentiometer give a voltage that can be anywhere between 0 and 3.3 V [volts].
  • The ADC (analog-to-digital converter) turns that voltage into a number from 0 to 4095 [counts], because it is 12 bits.
  • analogRead(pin) gives you that number. About 1.65 V reads roughly 2048, the middle.

What range does analogRead give on an ESP32?

  1. 0 to 255
  2. 0 to 4095
  3. 0 to 1023

The ESP32's ADC is 12-bit, so it reads 0 to 4095.

  • Why is my reading not exactly linear? The ESP32's ADC is less accurate near 0 V and near 3.3 V; for precise work, calibrate or use the middle of the range.

Reading a potentiometer

int raw = analogRead(34);          // 0..4095
float volts = raw * 3.3 / 4095;
Serial.println(volts);

GPIO 34 is input only: good for sensors

  • Connect the potentiometer's ends to 3.3 V and GND and its middle pin to GPIO 34 [pin34].
  • analogRead gives the raw number; multiply by 3.3 and divide by 4095 to get volts [code].
  • Turn the knob and watch the numbers change in the Serial Monitor.

analogRead returns 4095. About what voltage is on the pin?

  1. 3.3 V
  2. 0 V
  3. 5 V

4095 is the top of the range, which is about 3.3 V.

PWM: fake a voltage by blinking fast

  • A pin can only be fully on or fully off. PWM switches it on and off thousands of times a second.
  • The share of time it is on is the duty cycle [duty]. Our eyes see an LED on 25% of the time as dim [low] and 75% as bright [high].
  • analogWrite(pin, value) with value 0 to 255 sets the duty cycle on an ESP32 (the chip's LEDC unit does it).

analogWrite(2, 128) gives roughly what brightness?

  1. Off
  2. Full
  3. About half

128 of 255 is about 50% duty cycle, so about half brightness.

  • Is PWM real analog output? No, it is fast switching; for LEDs and motors the effect is the same as a lower voltage.

Tap the function that dims an LED.

What you learned

  • The ESP32's ADC turns 0 to 3.3 V into 0 to 4095.
  • raw × 3.3 ÷ 4095 converts a reading to volts.
  • PWM switches a pin fast; the duty cycle sets brightness or speed.
