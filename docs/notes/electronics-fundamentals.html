ALTROBYTE LAB · EMBEDDED FOUNDATION · LESSON 1
Electronics fundamentals: feel what happens in the wire.

Before the first line of firmware: voltage, current, resistance, Ohm's law, power, capacitors and coils. Fourteen short parts, each with something to try.

01What is actually moving

Every sensor reading, motor and Wi-Fi packet is the same thing underneath: electrons pushed through a material.

Random motion vs driftConnect the battery and watch the electrons
battery off
Battery off: the free electrons wander in every direction. Nothing useful happens.
  • Copper holds its outer electrons loosely: they wander randomly.
  • A battery adds a slight, steady drift in one direction. That drift is current.
  • One coulomb ≈ 6.24 × 10¹⁸ electrons; one coulomb per second is one ampere.
  • An ESP32 GPIO pin is happy at 10–20 mA: 0.010–0.020 coulombs every second.

02Voltage, current, resistance

Three quantities, one relationship. The water tank shows all three at once.

Water tank ↔ circuitMove both sliders and watch the flow
10.0 mA
More pressure or a wider pipe both give more flow.
Voltage (V), the push: a difference in pressure between two points. Current (I), the flow: charge per second. Resistance (R), the opposition: it turns lost energy into heat.
Remember: voltage is always between two points. There is no voltage "at" a pin without a reference, usually ground.

03A circuit must be a complete loop

Water can spill out of an open pipe. Charge cannot: it must come back to the source.

Close the loopOpen and close the switch
open
The switch is open: the loop is broken, so no current flows and the bulb stays dark.
  • No complete path back to the source = no current, whatever the voltage.
  • A real battery empties (its capacity); an ideal supply does not. Separate topic.
  • Use the water analogy to build intuition, then drop it: from here we speak in volts and amps.

04Reading a real resistor

The colour bands are the value. Learn to read them and stop guessing at the bench.

Colour code decoderBuild 220 Ω from the bands
1.0 kΩ ±5%
Task: make 220 Ω. Then try 1 kΩ and 10 kΩ.
  • Bands 1 and 2 are digits, band 3 the multiplier (how many zeros), band 4 the tolerance.
  • Hold it with the tolerance band (usually gold) on your right.
  • Red red brown = 2, 2, ×10 = 220 Ω. Brown black red = 1, 0, ×100 = 1 kΩ.

06Ohm's law

One line ties the three together. If you remember nothing else today, remember this.

V = I × R  ·  I = V ÷ R  ·  R = V ÷ I
Solver and V–I graphTask: what current flows with 3.3 V across 330 Ω?
10.0 mA
The slope of the line is set by the resistance: smaller R, steeper line, more current.
Ohm's law works for resistors. An LED or diode is not ohmic: its drop stays roughly fixed (about 2 V for red), which is why we subtract it first.

07The LED on your board

A red LED drops about 2 V and wants about 10 mA. Your ESP32 pin gives 3.3 V. Which resistor?

R = (3.3 V − 2.0 V) ÷ 0.010 A = 130 Ω
Choose the series resistorWatch the LED and the current
-
Pick a resistor and see what happens.
  • Nearest standard values above 130 Ω: 150 Ω and 220 Ω. Pick 220 Ω: slightly dimmer, safer pin, in every kit.
  • No resistor at all: the current spikes, the LED and the pin can die.

08Power and heat

Current through a resistance always makes heat. Every part you burn out is this equation ignored.

P = V × I = I² × R = V² ÷ R
Burn a resistor (safely)Pick its rating, then raise the voltage
cool
50mA
0.25watts
Power is the rate electrical energy becomes something else: in a resistor, all of it becomes heat.
  • Watts = joules per second. A motor turns most of it into motion, an LED into light, a resistor into heat.
  • Always pick a resistor rated for more than the power it will carry (¼ W is the usual small one).

09Battery life is power discipline

A battery IoT node lives or dies on its average current.

How long will the battery last?2000 mAh cell: change how the node behaves
-
Always awake at 80 mA, a 2000 mAh cell lasts about a day. Now try waking only 1 % of the time.
  • Average current = awake current × awake share + sleep current × sleep share.
  • Same hardware: about a day always on, years when it sleeps at 50 µA between readings.

10Capacitance

A capacitor stores charge and resists sudden changes in voltage.

τ = R × C  ·  63 % after 1τ  ·  about 99 % after 5τ
Charging and dischargingPress charge; change R or C
τ = 1.00 s
0.00 V
A bigger R or a bigger C means a slower curve.
Capacitance (C), in farads: charge held per volt. Real parts are µF, nF and pF. Two plates, an insulator between; remove the source and the charge stays for a while.

11Why every chip has a capacitor next to it

When a motor kicks in, the supply dips. Without a capacitor, your microcontroller resets.

12Inductance and the relay spike

An inductor resists a change in current, and gets violent when you ignore it.

The relay spikeSwitch the coil on, then off; then fit the flyback diode
0 V
Any coil is an inductor: relays, solenoids, motor windings, buzzers.
  • Switch on: the current ramps up, it does not jump.
  • Switch off: the collapsing field tries to keep current flowing; with nowhere to go it makes a big reverse spike that kills transistors.
  • A flyback diode across the coil gives that current a safe path. Every relay board has one.

13Quick check

Five questions. Answer honestly: it tells you what to revisit.

1. A multimeter shows "3.3 V". What is missing from that statement?

Voltage is always between two points, usually a pin and ground.

2. 5 V across 1 kΩ. What current flows?

I = V ÷ R = 5 ÷ 1000 = 0.005 A = 5 mA.

3. A green LED drops 2.1 V and wants 12 mA on a 5 V rail. Closest resistor?

(5 − 2.1) ÷ 0.012 ≈ 242 Ω; use the next standard value up.

4. Why is a 0.1 µF capacitor placed next to every chip?

That is decoupling: it keeps the chip from resetting or glitching.

5. A relay coil is switched off by a transistor. What protects the transistor?

The diode gives the coil's current a path, so there is no spike.

14Carry these five into the next lesson

  • Voltage is a difference between two points. Always ask "relative to what?"
  • Current does the work, and current kills components.
  • V = I × R is the whole first month in three letters.
  • A capacitor smooths voltage; an inductor fights changes in current.
  • Power becomes heat: check the rating before you power up.
