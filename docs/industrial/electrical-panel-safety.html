02How electricity hurts

It is the current through your body that hurts, and it takes very little. Our 230 V and 415 V are more than enough to kill.

Drill · Current through the bodySlide to see what each current does
1 mA
CurrentWhat happens
1 mAA tingle
10 mAMuscles lock: you cannot let go of the wire
30 mABreathing becomes difficult; this is why an RCCB trips at 30 mA
100 mAThe heart goes out of rhythm (fibrillation): death within minutes
1 A and moreBurns inside the body, the heart stops
Example: wet hands and wet floor in a cooling-water area lower the body's resistance. The same 230 V that gives a tingle with dry hands can push a deadly current with wet ones. That is why rubber mats and dry hands matter.

03Arc flash: the explosion in the panel

When a short circuit jumps through the air, it makes an arc hotter than the surface of the sun, with a blast and molten copper.

Arc flash: a fault through air between phases or to earth. It reaches around 20,000 °C in a split second, throws molten metal, a pressure wave and blinding light. Most arc flashes happen when someone is working on or near a live panel: a dropped spanner, a loose cover, a wrong test lead.

04Test before touch: live, dead, live

A switch in the OFF position does not prove a circuit is dead. Only a test does, done the right way.

Drill · Prove it deadTap the steps in the right order
Not proven
Feeder 7 (pump motor) must be worked on. Prove it is dead.
  1. Isolate and lock the feeder MCCB with your own lock and tag.
  2. Test the tester on a known live source (or a proving unit): the tester works.
  3. Test the circuit phase to phase and each phase to earth: no voltage.
  4. Test the tester again on the known live source: it still works, so the "dead" reading was real.
  5. Earth the conductors if the work needs it, then start work.
VFDs and capacitors: after switch-off, wait the time written on the drive (often 5 to 15 minutes) and test the DC bus too. "It is off" has killed people who did not wait.

05Protection devices and earthing

Each protection device watches for one kind of fault. Knowing which one trips tells you what went wrong.

Drill · What trips?Pick a fault, then the device that should clear it
Choose a fault
Pick a fault first.
FaultCleared by
Short circuit (phase to phase)MCB / MCCB / fuse, instantly
Overload (motor drawing too much for too long)Overload relay or the breaker's thermal trip

Earthing: every metal body (panel, motor frame, cable tray) is connected to the earth pit. If a live wire touches the body, the current goes to earth and the protection trips, instead of going through you. A broken earth wire makes the body a trap.

06Panel room rules

Most panel incidents start with something small in the room around the panel.

Doors closedPanel doors and covers closed and bolted. An open door is an arc flash waiting to happen.
Nothing storedNo cartons, oil, cloth or tools in the panel room. 1 metre clear in front of every panel.
Rubber matsInsulating mats in front of the panels, dry and undamaged.
Heat and dustSteel plant dust is conductive. Filters clean, AC working, panels sealed (IP54 or better).
Thermal scansHot spots on loose terminals show up on a thermal camera months before they burn.
Permit to workNo work on a panel without an electrical permit, isolation and the test.
Example: a rolling mill MCC tripped every afternoon. A thermal scan found a busbar joint at 95 °C: a loose bolt. Tightened in a planned shutdown, it saved a fire and a three-day stoppage.

07Test

Eight questions. Score 70% or more (6 of 8) to earn your Electrical Panel Safety certificate.

1. A feeder switch is in the OFF position. Is the motor circuit safe to touch?

  • A. Yes, it is off
  • B. Only after lock, tag and a live-dead-live test
  • C. Yes, if the motor is not running
Answer: B. OFF is not proof; only the test is.

2. Why do you test the tester again after the circuit reads dead?

  • A. To use up time
  • B. To prove the tester did not fail during the test
  • C. It is not needed
Answer: B. A failed tester also reads "dead".

3. Which current through the body can stop the heart?

  • A. 1 mA
  • B. 100 mA
  • C. Only above 10 A
Answer: B. Around 100 mA causes fibrillation.

4. A person touches a live wire and current leaks to earth through them. What trips?

  • A. The overload relay
  • B. The RCCB / ELCB at 30 mA
  • C. Nothing
Answer: B. That is the job of the RCCB.

5. You switched off a VFD. When can you work on it?

  • A. Immediately
  • B. After the waiting time on the drive and testing the DC bus
  • C. After the fan stops
Answer: B. The capacitors stay charged.

6. What is an arc flash?

  • A. A spark from a welding rod
  • B. A fault through air with extreme heat, blast and molten metal
  • C. A light on the panel
Answer: B.

7. Cartons are kept in front of a panel to save space. What is wrong?

  • A. Nothing
  • B. The panel needs 1 metre clear; cartons are fuel and block escape
  • C. Only that it looks untidy
Answer: B.

8. Why must the motor frame be earthed?

  • A. To make it heavier
  • B. So a fault goes to earth and trips the protection, not through a person
  • C. It is only for lightning
Answer: B.
Recap. Know the panel: incomer, busbars, feeders, contactors, overloads, drives. 100 mA can kill; RCCB at 30 mA. Arc flash is an explosion: stay outside the boundary unless authorised and dressed for it. Lock, tag, live-dead-live. Wait for drives to discharge. Earth every metal body. Panel rooms clean, dry, closed and clear.
