01Why safety comes first

Every rule in a steel plant was written after someone got hurt. Learning them is how it never happens to you.

Most injuries do not come from big disasters. They come from small shortcuts: a glove not worn "just for two minutes", a guard removed to clear a jam quickly, walking under a crane load because it is the shorter way. The work gets done, nothing happens, so the shortcut becomes a habit. One day it does not work.

Think of a helmet on a bike. You do not wear it because you plan to fall. You wear it because you cannot choose the day you fall.
Your three rights on the shop floor: the right to know the hazards of your job, the right to proper safety equipment, and the right to stop work when something is unsafe, without being punished for it.
Zero harm is the target in every good plant: not "fewer injuries", none. Production can be made up tomorrow. A lost finger cannot.

The six hazards in this bay, in words:

  1. Standing under a crane load. Never walk or stand under a suspended load.
  2. Oil on the walkway. Cover with absorbent, barricade, report.
  3. Machine guard open. Guards stay closed; a jam is cleared only after lockout-tagout.
  4. Fire extinguisher blocked. Keep one metre clear around every extinguisher and exit.
  5. Ladder too steep and not held. 1 out for every 4 up, a second person holds it.
  6. Cable across the walkway. Route it overhead or under a cable cover.

Gotcha: gloves near a drill, lathe or any rotating part are dangerous. The glove gets caught and pulls the hand in. Near rotating machines: no gloves, no loose sleeves, no chains, hair tied.

04Lockout-Tagout: make the machine safe before you touch it

Before anyone repairs, cleans or clears a jam inside a machine, every source of energy is switched off, locked and tagged.

Lockout = your own padlock on the isolation point, the key stays in your pocket. Tagout = a tag on it with your name, date and the reason.
Drill · Isolate the conveyorTap the six steps in the right order
Machine: RUNNING
A jam inside the conveyor. Make it safe, step by step.
  1. Tell the operator and the supervisor that the machine is going down.
  2. Stop the machine the normal way.
  3. Isolate every energy source: electrical, air, hydraulic, and also springs and gravity (a raised part can drop).
  4. Lock and tag each isolation point with your own lock. Two people working = two locks.
  5. Release stored energy: bleed the air, lower raised parts, discharge capacitors.
  6. Try to start it. If it does not start, it is safe. Put the switch back to OFF.
Golden rule: only the person who put the lock removes it. Never remove someone else's lock, never work under someone else's lock.

05Steel plant hazards: hot metal, cranes and gas

Three things make a steel plant different from any other factory. Respect them every single day.

🔥 Hot metal and slagLiquid iron is around 1,500 °C. A drop of water under it turns to steam instantly and throws metal out. Keep moulds, ladles and tools bone dry; stay behind the marked line during tapping.
🏗 EOT cranesNever stand or walk under a load. Only a trained signaller gives signals, one person, clear hand signs. Check slings and hooks before every lift.
☁ GasBlast furnace and coke oven gas contain carbon monoxide (CO). It has no colour and no smell, and it kills. Gas areas need a CO monitor, a permit and a buddy.

06Fire: the right extinguisher, the right way

A fire needs fuel, heat and oxygen. Take away any one and it dies. The wrong extinguisher can make it worse.

Fight a fire only if it is small, you have the right extinguisher and your exit is behind you. Otherwise: raise the alarm, leave, go to the assembly point. Never use a lift in a fire.

08Hazards and near misses: speak up early

A near miss is an accident that did not happen this time. Reporting it is how the next one is stopped.

Hazard: anything that can hurt someone (oil on the floor, a missing guard, a blocked exit). Near miss: something that almost hurt someone (a part fell and missed a person, a forklift braked just in time).

Studies of many workplaces found this pattern: for every serious injury there are hundreds of near misses before it. Fix the near misses and the serious injury never arrives.

  1. See it: notice what is unsafe.
  2. Make it safe if you can (wipe the oil, put a barricade), or keep people away.
  3. Report it to your supervisor or in the near-miss book, the same shift.

10Test

Eight questions. Score 70% or more (6 of 8) to earn your Safety Induction certificate.

1. You need to clear a jam inside a conveyor. What do you do first?

  • A. Wear gloves and clear it quickly
  • B. Lockout-tagout: stop, isolate, lock with your own lock, try to start it
  • C. Ask the operator to keep an eye on the start button
Answer: B. Only your own lock guarantees nobody can start it.

2. An oil drum catches fire. Which extinguisher must you never use?

  • A. Water
  • B. Foam
  • C. CO₂
Answer: A. Water spreads burning oil.

3. An EOT crane is carrying a coil across the bay. What do you do?

  • A. Walk quickly under it
  • B. Wait or go around; never stand or walk under a load
  • C. Signal the crane operator yourself
Answer: B. Only the trained signaller signals, and nobody goes under a load.

4. Your CO monitor reads 80 ppm. What do you do?

  • A. Finish the job quickly
  • B. Leave at once, upwind, and raise the alarm
  • C. Open a window and continue
Answer: B. Above 50 ppm is danger: leave and alert.

5. A colleague has collapsed in a gas area. What do you do?

  • A. Run in and pull them out
  • B. Raise the alarm and call the gas rescue team with breathing sets
  • C. Splash water on their face
Answer: B. Do not become the second victim.

6. A ladder is 4 metres high where it rests. How far from the wall should its foot be?

  • A. About 1 metre
  • B. Touching the wall
  • C. About 3 metres
Answer: A. One out for every four up.

7. You are working on a lathe. Which is right?

  • A. Wear thick gloves
  • B. No gloves, sleeves tight, hair tied, safety glasses on
  • C. Loose clothes for comfort
Answer: B. Gloves and loose clothes get caught in rotating parts.

8. A spanner falls from a platform and lands near a worker. Nobody is hurt. What is it?

  • A. Nothing happened, nothing to do
  • B. A near miss: report it the same shift
  • C. The worker's fault
Answer: B. Reporting near misses prevents serious injuries.
